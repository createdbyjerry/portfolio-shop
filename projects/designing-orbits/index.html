Designing Orbits

Making Rocket Science Child's Play

Astrodynamics is notoriously mind-bending. We built an interactive learning platform that turned complex orbital physics into an intuitive, real-time simulation sandbox. Think less "staring at terrifying equations" and more "playing God with gravity." The result? A massive jump in student comprehension and a shiny new subscription revenue model for the business.

  • RoleSr. Product Designer
  • CompanySlingshot
  • IndustryAerospace / Defense
  • Year2020–2021
  • TimelineSep – Mar
01 / The Problem

Houston, We Have a Usability Issue

We were fighting a three-front war against boredom, technical debt, and rigid legacy software:

Human Challenge

Students were drowning in dry, static textbooks. Instructors were exhausted from trying to explain complex 3D orbital trajectories using 2D whiteboards.

Dev Challenge

Building a real-time orbital simulation with a team of just four engineers is a massive lift. We had to balance absolute scientific accuracy with butter-smooth UI performance without melting anyone's laptop.

Business Challenge

Slingshot needed to launch an educational platform that universities would actually pay for, proving that a subscription model for space education could net recurring revenue.

Why it mattered

If the interface was too simple, it was a toy. If it was too complex, it was homework. We had to hit the absolute sweet spot where intense physics met effortless UX.

02 / The Launch Constraints

We Didn't Have NASA's Budget

We didn’t exactly have NASA’s timeline or budget. We were flying with:

A Lean Crew

4 engineers handling both the heavy-lifting math backend and the frontend pixels.

Strict Flight Paths

A tight 3-month MVP window.

Zero Margin for Error

We partnered with two astrodynamics subject matter experts (SMEs) to ensure our physics weren’t just "close enough"—they had to be mathematically perfect.

03 / Strategy

The "Teach Them to Retain Them" North Star

Our experience principles were simple: ditch the flash, prioritize friction-free experimentation, and make gravity tangible.

We bet heavily on two ideas:

Students don’t learn by reading about orbits; they learn by bending them.

If we made a tool that saved professors from lecturing into a void, adoption would follow naturally.

05 / UI Design

The Space-Glass Aesthetic

To make the platform feel like a modern control room rather than a spreadsheet, we built a sleek, systemized visual language:

The Vibe

High-contrast orbit paths overlaid on deep-space dark backgrounds, using "space glass" containers to layer complex data beautifully.

The Machinery

We built a modular library of sliders, playback controls, and visual indicators.

The Secret Weapon

Tight Figma token systems for color, typography, and motion easing meant our 4-person dev team could implement features with zero guesswork and maximum speed.

07 / Splashdown

The Impact

We didn't just launch a pretty tool; we shifted the trajectory of how this subject is taught.

Student Engagement

More than 85% of enrolled students actively spent their time tinkering in the platform during semester one.

Lightbulb Moments

Professors reported a massive 60% improvement in student comprehension of orbital mechanics.

The Bottom Line

Secured early institutional subscriptions, validating the business model and proving the MVP's worth.

The Horizon

The design system and simulation framework we built were so scalable they are already being used as the foundation for future AR/VR expansion.

The ultimate takeaway

Empathy-driven design can make even literal rocket science accessible. When you bridge the gap between brilliant engineers, precise scientists, and human-centric UX, everyone wins.
